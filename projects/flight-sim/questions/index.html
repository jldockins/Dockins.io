Switching the reference aircraft from the 152 to a 172 — why, and what does it change?

open2026-09-01

Raised by me. No reason for the switch has been recorded yet — that is the first half of the question and it needs answering before anything moves, because the 152 was itself a deliberate change from the T182T on 25 Aug and reversing course twice in a week is how a build stops converging.

Two candidate reasons, and they lead different places (raised in conversation, not yet my position):

  • The aircraft actually being purchased is a 172. That is a premise change. The non-goal gets rewritten to say 172, AircraftLimits.h gets 172 numbers, and the project continues unchanged in shape.
  • A 172 is the better platform to instruct in. That is the instructing half of the motivation starting to drive hardware decisions rather than only the right-seat constraint — worth naming as such, because it would be the first time the CFII goal moved a build choice.

Which of these it is determines whether this is a one-line premise edit or a shift in what the Why section is steering by.

This crosses a stated non-goal. The third non-goal reads: "Not a 182 or anything larger — the reference aircraft is a 152 and stays a 152. Bigger airframes bring back the prop control, cowl flaps and turbo that the 152 decision deliberately removed." A 172 is larger than a 152. Recording it as an open question rather than quietly building it is the point; if the switch happens, that non-goal gets rewritten deliberately, not ignored. Note the stated rationale mostly survives a 172 — the common 172s are still four-cylinder, carburetted or injected, fixed-pitch, no cowl flaps, no turbo — so the non-goal's reasoning may not actually exclude a 172 even though its wording does. That is a wording bug worth fixing either way.

What it changes, cheaply: less than the T182T reversal did. The architecture is aircraft-agnostic; the six-pack decision, the panel-overlay approach, the AOC scale work, the preview tooling and sim-check all carry over untouched. Crucially the shape of the engine model survives — four cylinders, fixed-pitch, no manifold pressure — which is exactly what the 182-to-152 change had to demolish.

What it changes, expensively:

  • Every number in AircraftLimits.h again. They are currently wrong for the 152 and would become wrong for a 172 differently. This is the second full invalidation of that header in a week.
  • The current Next action — "rewrite AircraftLimits.h for the 152" — is at risk of being written twice. That is the concrete cost of leaving this open, and the reason to settle it before touching the header.
  • The whole "Awaiting verification" list resets: airspeed arcs, engine limits, usable fuel, electrical voltage, panel dimensions.
  • Panel geometry improves. The 152's narrow panel is recorded as a tight fit for the GTN 650Xi / GNC 255A / GTX 345 stack; a 172 panel is wider, so that pressure eases.
  • Possibly the CFII framing. Whether instrument training actually happens in a 152 or a 172 bears on the motivation, and the 152 was chosen partly as "what CFII instruction happens in".

Which 172, and it matters: the arcs and fuel differ enough across variants that "a 172" is not a specification. Broad shape of the split — a 172M/N/P is a carburetted O-320 with carb heat, close to the 152's control set; a 172R/S is a fuel-injected IO-360 with no carb heat, higher Vne and roughly double the usable fuel. Picking the wrong one means the carb-heat control either exists or doesn't. No figure here is from a POH, so all of it stays VERIFY until one is in hand — which is the same document gap already blocking the 152 numbers.

Right seat — legible, or a full working position?

open2026-09-01

Raised by Claude while writing the Why section, and agreed as a real gap rather than a wording problem.

The Who it's for line now says me — a single-user rig — and the right-seat constraint is reconciled with that by saying the instructor in that seat is one teaching me, not a student I'm teaching. That holds today. But the same section's motivation says "practice now, instruct later", and instructing inverts the seats: student in the left, me in the right. The two halves of Why imply different rigs.

What's actually at stake is panel geometry, and the constraint already says so — right-seat legibility and control reach are requirements "not nice-to-haves", affecting geometry from Phase 3 onward. The open part is how far that goes:

  • Legible only (today's reading): the right seat has to read the panel at an angle. That's what already rules out the TN CYD panels for anything scanned, and what makes the IPS AOC the right carrier for the six-pack.
  • Full working position: the right seat also has to fly it — reach the yoke, throttle, mixture, carb heat, and work the radios. That's a much stronger claim. It likely means centring the panel between the seats rather than at my own eye height, and it may mean duplicated or centrally-placed engine controls.

The distinction matters now rather than later because Phase 3 cuts the panel, and panel geometry is the one thing that isn't cheap to redo — unlike the overlay, which is a cut sheet. Getting this wrong means cutting twice.

Bearing on it: whether the CFII actually happens, and whether anyone else ever sits in the rig. "Just me" was chosen deliberately, so the honest reading may be that the full working position is a later requirement, and the question is whether to build geometry that accommodates it now or accept a rebuild then.

Does the sim PC have to be bought, or is there a machine on hand that can run X-Plane at a usable frame rate?

open2026-09-01

This is the single biggest budget risk and it is unanswered. The ceiling is $1,000 total (soft, but a decision that would blow it gets flagged). Everything received so far — the CYD boards, the AOC monitor, the vinyl cutter — was already on hand and cost this project nothing. So the whole ceiling is still notionally available, and a purchased simulator PC capable of X-Plane at a usable frame rate would consume most of it.

The consequence, not just the cost: if it's a purchase, Phases 3–4 get squeezed — the panel material, yoke, rudder pedals, encoders, switches and wiring are all still unchosen and all still unpaid. Those are the phases where the build stops being firmware and becomes a cockpit.

What bears on it and isn't known yet:

  • Whether an existing machine can do it at all. "Usable frame rate" needs a number before this is testable, and the platform question (X-Plane vs MSFS) is itself unresolved — MSFS is the heavier target, so answering the platform question first may answer this one.
  • The bridge is deliberately transport-agnostic and the pages don't know their data source, so the sim PC can be decided late without invalidating firmware work. That is what makes deferring this safe; it is not a reason to leave it unanswered, because the budget for Phases 3–4 can't be planned until it's settled.

The CYD boards shift off-axis — what are they actually for?

open2026-09-01

The three ESP32-2432S028R boards are "must use" hardware, and until now the working assumption was that they'd carry instruments. That collides with the right-seat constraint. Their 2.8-inch panels are TN, which shifts noticeably off-axis; an instructor in the right seat reads the panel at an angle, so anything that has to be scanned from that seat is a poor fit for those boards. The AOC 16T20 is IPS and holds contrast off-axis, which is why it already carries the display-only instruments.

That rules out a use rather than supplying one. The framing that survives is: give the CYDs things a pilot glances at rather than scans, where a shifted grey level costs nothing because the information is presence/absence or a single value read deliberately. Candidates on the table:

  • Annunciators — discrete lamps. Legibility off-axis barely matters when the signal is "something is lit".
  • A standby instrument — read deliberately, and only when the primary is gone.
  • The audio panel — but note these boards are touch, and the working rule is that if a pilot touches it, prefer a physical control. An audio panel is touched constantly.

Bearing on it: the deferred G5 vs GI 275 question. If the glass retrofit happens, a CYD is a near-scale stand-in for a G5 and the calculus changes; if it doesn't, the CYDs go back to being preview devices. So this may not be answerable before the aircraft purchase.
